The name

The Greeks had two words for time. Chronos is the measured kind: hours, sprints, the calendar. Kairos is the other kind, the opportune moment, the narrow opening when action works. Kairos the app is built for that: it keeps the hours of work running in the background, so you can be present for the moments that matter.

What it does

One workspace. Every model, all your memory.

01

Chat with any model

Claude, Codex, local models through Ollama or the built-in engine, or any OpenAI-compatible API. Choose the model and its reasoning level for each conversation, and watch a meter of how full the context is.

02

A memory you own

Your knowledge is a vault of plain Markdown files. Search it, browse it as a linked map, and edit it in Kairos or any other tool. It stays useful without the app.

03

Agents that remember

Named agents with their own role, goals and memory. They work through a board of cards, ask you when they need a decision, and can team up on bigger jobs.

04

Work on a schedule

Scheduled prompts and built-in routines like a daily brief. Each runs once, can be stopped, and delivers its result here or to a connected channel.

05

Every run, step by step

Open any chat, task or agent run and see what it did: each tool it called, how long it took, what failed and how it ended.

06

Back up everything

One file holds your chats, notes, agents and settings. Restore it on any machine, with a safety copy of what was there. Saved passwords stay out unless you choose.

Everything in Kairos

The whole toolkit. Nothing to bolt on.

Conversation

  • Claude, Codex, local and API models, chosen per chat
  • Attachments, folder-scoped workspaces and projects
  • @ references to files, notes and other chats
  • Previews for documents, spreadsheets, slides, PDFs and code
  • A side browser, sandboxed from the app
  • Screen capture straight into a chat (Windows)
  • Voice input and spoken replies
  • Up to three read-only helpers working in parallel
  • Find in chat, summaries and a terminal-style view

Knowledge and your day

  • A Markdown vault with search and a linked map
  • Notes and priorities, kept in sync with the vault
  • Library for documents and chat files
  • Calendar with CalDAV and iCal sync
  • Email through IMAP and SMTP
  • Google Workspace sign-in
  • A Home dashboard of what's next

Agents and automation

  • Persistent agents with memory, goals and an inbox
  • Agent teams that divide up larger work
  • Scheduled tasks and built-in routines
  • Webhook triggers that start work from outside
  • Lifecycle hooks on tools, replies and runs
  • Run timelines for every chat, task and agent

Tools and skills

  • Tool Store for reusable skills
  • A catalog of MCP tool servers, with sign-in
  • Health checks, and new tools held until you approve them
  • Cookbook to download and run local models
  • Custom tabs you build in Developer Mode

Channels and access

  • Talk to Kairos and its agents from Discord
  • Telegram, Slack, Signal, Matrix, Mattermost, IRC, iMessage, email and ntfy connectors
  • Remote access from your phone over Tailscale, with HTTPS and a login
  • A usage notch for your Claude and Codex limits (Windows)

Safety and control

  • App-wide permissions, asked once and remembered
  • Code runs in a sandbox, and you review its changes
  • Agents and tasks kept to their own folders
  • File checkpoints with rollback
  • Saved passwords encrypted on your machine
  • Optional accounts with two-factor sign-in
  • Logs, full backup and restore
  • Updates itself on Windows

Before you install

The installers aren't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen and macOS Gatekeeper may ask you to approve the app the first time. The Mac build runs on Apple Silicon, not Intel Macs. Screenshots use demo data.

Kairos runs on your computer. When you choose a cloud model or connect an outside service, the data that request needs goes to that provider.

Questions

Good to know.

Is Kairos free?

Yes. Kairos is free and open source under the MIT license. Models you connect may have their own costs: a Claude or Codex subscription, or an API account. Local models cost nothing to run.

Where does my data live?

On your computer. Chats, settings and agents are in your app data folder, and your memory is a folder of Markdown files you choose. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you connect a cloud model or an outside service.

Which models work?

Claude through Claude Code, Codex through the Codex CLI, local models through Ollama or the built-in engine, and any OpenAI-compatible API. You can switch per conversation.

Does it update itself?

On Windows, yes: updates download in the background and install when you quit. On macOS, download the new version from this page and replace the app.

Can I reach it from my phone?

Yes, through Tailscale, set up in Settings. It's never exposed to the public internet: it serves HTTPS on your private network and requires a login.

Can I back up and move my data?

Yes. Settings has a full backup that captures everything in one file, and a restore that keeps a safety copy of what it replaces.

What happened to JARVIS?

JARVIS is now Kairos. The update keeps your install, your chats and your settings exactly where they were. On a Mac, after installing Kairos you can delete the old JARVIS app.
